# LLM decode benchmark

gpt-oss-20b (MXFP4) decoding one sequence, the experts offloaded to host
memory as each engine runs a 20B MoE on a 12 GB card: tokens a second at two
context depths.

## Running

    ./run.sh

One command downloads the model (pinned to its Hugging Face revision, its
sha256 checked), builds llama.cpp's `llama-bench` at its pinned release
(b11900, CUDA) and base's decode artifact (`lean/algorithms/GptOss/Decode.lean`),
then measures and writes `REPORT.md`.

The engines run alone and without the memory cap: each keeps about 10 GB of
experts resident in host memory, so nothing else may run beside them, and
never the two at once. Builds run under the cap.

## What is measured

- **llama.cpp**: its offload sweep (`-ncmoe`, the layers whose experts stay
  on the CPU) at the deepest context, then its fastest setting that fits
  at each depth: llama-bench's prompt (512 tokens) and decode (128 tokens)
  rates, the mean of 3 runs with their standard deviation.
- **base**: `applications/gpt-oss/bench.py` at the same depths: the depth's
  tokens held (put through the prefill untimed), then a 512-token prompt
  through the prefill, timed, then 128 generated by one turn, the
  artifact's own loop as a chat runs it, after the prompt (a context 512
  deeper than llama-bench's decode), in a context of the depth, the prompt
  and the generation.
  Base multiplies by the same weights llama.cpp does: the GGUF's Q8_0
  attention and output head (`applications/gpt-oss/prepare_q8.py` appends
  them to the bank, checked against the bf16 checkpoint) and the MXFP4
  experts; its expert pool sits in pinned host memory, every slot the card
  has room for filled at start-up.

## Where base stands, and why

Base decodes at 1.00x of llama.cpp at 512 and 0.93x at 8192 (its decode
512 tokens deeper than llama.cpp's). What is left at depth:
- **attention**, the fused tile kernel at about 140 GB/s against the
  card's 333;
- **a few misses a token**: beside the 8K cache the card holds 754 of the
  768 experts, so the host waits on the router each layer to keep its cache
  and a miss crosses the bus;
- **the experts** at about 68% of the card's bandwidth
  (`applications/gpt-oss/kernel_bench.py`).

Base's prompt runs at 0.98x of llama.cpp at 512 and 0.62x at 8192. The
prefill (`lean/algorithms/GptOss/Prefill.lean`) puts a chunk of up to 512
tokens through each layer at once, its projections and experts on the int8
tensor cores (the wide `mma`, m16n8k32, MXFP4 and Q8_0 weights against
activations quantized per 32-block). What is left is attention at depth:
llama.cpp's flash attention runs on f16 tensor cores, 31 ms in all for a
prompt at 8192 (ncu on llama-bench), where base's runs in fp32 on the CUDA
cores, about ten times that.
